Week 6 interview Q&A: data quality and governance

1. How do you define and measure data quality? By dimensions tied to use: completeness, validity, uniqueness, consistency, timeliness, accuracy. For each critical dataset agree with consumers on measurable rules and thresholds, track pass rate over time, and tie severity to business impact. Follow-ups: Which dimension is hardest and why? (Accuracy: needs an external truth.) How do you prioritize which tables to monitor? (Usage via lineage/query logs, business criticality, blast radius.)

2. Where in a pipeline do you place checks? At ingestion (schema, basic validity, quarantine), after key transforms (assertions on invariants: row counts after joins, uniqueness), and at the serving layer (freshness, reconciliation to source). Early checks fail cheaply; late checks protect consumers. Follow-ups: Block vs warn? (Block when wrong data is worse than late; otherwise quarantine/warn.) Write-Audit-Publish? (Stage, audit, atomically publish.)

3. A table loads daily; yesterday the row count was 40% lower and no job failed. How would you have caught it, and what now? Volume anomaly detection against a seasonal baseline (median/MAD by weekday). Now: check upstream freshness and partial extract, compare to source counts, quarantine the partition, notify consumers, backfill, add the missing check/contract. Follow-ups: Why median/MAD over mean/stdev? (Robust to earlier outages and heavy tails.) False positives on holidays? (Calendar features, manual suppression, wider thresholds.)

4. Freshness vs timeliness vs latency? Freshness: age of the newest data. Timeliness: availability by the agreed time (SLO). Latency: delay of one record through the pipeline. Measure freshness from event time when possible, not job-finish time (a job can succeed on stale input). Follow-ups: How to distinguish "no new data" from "pipeline broken"? (Heartbeat/synthetic rows, source-side counters, separate ingestion lag metric.)

5. How do you detect distribution drift? Compare current vs reference windows: PSI or KS for numerics, category share shifts (chi-square/JS divergence), null-rate and cardinality changes. Pick the reference deliberately (last week vs training set). Alert on sustained drift, not single-day noise. Follow-ups: PSI thresholds? (<0.1/0.25 are rules of thumb.) Bin choice effects? (Quantile bins from the reference are more stable.)

6. dbt tests vs Great Expectations vs Soda vs Deequ? dbt tests: SQL-native, in the transformation DAG, simple generics; great for warehouse models. GE: Python expectation suites and docs, flexible for files/Spark. Soda: declarative YAML checks plus cloud UI. Deequ: Spark-scale, metrics-based constraint suggestion. Choose by stack and who writes checks; don't run four. (Check current docs.) Follow-ups: How do tests gate deployment? (dbt build stops downstream on failure; CI runs on slim/changed models.) Storing results? (Persist results to a table for trend and SLO reporting.)

7. What is a data contract and how is it enforced? A versioned agreement between producer and consumers covering schema, semantics, SLAs, ownership, PII tags and change policy. Enforced in CI (breaking change detection against a registry), at publish time (schema validation), and at runtime (checks/quarantine), with a deprecation process. Follow-ups: Who owns it? (Producer, with consumers consulted.) How is it different from a schema? (Adds semantics, SLAs, ownership, process.) Pitfall? (Contracts without enforcement or without producer buy-in.)

8. Explain backward vs forward vs full compatibility with an example. Backward: new schema reads old data; adding email with a default or deleting a field is OK. Forward: old schema reads new data; adding a field is OK (old readers ignore it), deleting needs the field to have had a default. Full: both. Which to choose depends on who upgrades first: backward -> consumers first; forward -> producers first. Follow-ups: Is renaming compatible? (No, unless aliases.) Why default BACKWARD in Schema Registry? (Allows rewinding consumers to old data.) Parquet/warehouse analogue? (Adding nullable column OK; type change often breaks.)

9. What is lineage and what do you use it for? A graph of how datasets and columns derive from one another (OpenLineage events, SQL parsing, dbt manifest). Impact analysis before changes, root-cause upstream search, compliance (PII flow), deprecation of unused assets, ownership routing of alerts. Follow-ups: Column-level vs table-level? (Column-level is costlier but needed for PII/impact.) Gaps? (Dynamic SQL, notebooks, external tools.)

10. SLI, SLO, SLA for a data product? SLI: "fraction of days the daily_sales table is complete by 07:00". SLO: 99% over 30 days. SLA: contractual commitment with penalties. Error budget decides whether to pause features for reliability work. Follow-ups: What do you do when you burn the budget? (Freeze risky changes, invest in checks/automation.) How to alert? (Burn-rate alerts rather than every miss.)

11. How do you reduce alert fatigue for data quality? Tier by severity and business impact, ownership routing, dedupe/grouping by root cause via lineage, thresholds tuned from history, auto-resolve, weekly review of noisy checks, and delete checks nobody acts on. Follow-ups: Metric for alert quality? (Actionable ratio, time to acknowledge/resolve.)

12. Describe a data incident process. Detect, declare severity, assign an incident lead, assess blast radius (lineage), mitigate (stop downstream publishing, rollback/restore, backfill), communicate to consumers with ETA, resolve, blameless postmortem with root cause and prevention (new check/contract/test), follow-up tracking. Follow-ups: How do you restore a bad overwrite? (Time travel/snapshots, rerun idempotent job from raw.) Communicating wrong numbers already consumed? (Proactive notice, versioned fix, restate reports.)

13. How would you implement GDPR right-to-erasure in a lake + warehouse + Kafka platform? Central subject registry/ID resolution; a deletion service fans requests out to every store; lakehouse DELETE/MERGE then compaction and snapshot expiry/VACUUM so old files disappear; warehouse deletes plus time-travel expiry; downstream marts/features rebuilt; Kafka: short retention, compaction with tombstones, or crypto-shredding with per-subject keys; backups age out with documented lag; audit evidence without PII; SLA to complete (one month under GDPR, check current law). Follow-ups: Immutable logs? (Crypto-shredding or avoid PII in logs.) Analytics needing history? (Pseudonymize/aggregate so deletion doesn't break metrics.) ML models trained on the data? (Policy question; retrain cadence; legal guidance.)

14. Masking vs tokenization vs hashing vs encryption? Masking: obscure display (irreversible, role-based or static copies). Tokenization: random surrogate with vault mapping, reversible for authorized services, preserves joins if deterministic. Hashing: one-way; use HMAC with secret since unsalted hashes of emails/phones can be brute-forced. Encryption: reversible with keys; protects at rest/in transit and field-level for high sensitivity. Follow-ups: Join across datasets without exposing email? (Deterministic HMAC/token with shared key under access control.) Is a hash of a user ID anonymous? (No, pseudonymous; still personal data under GDPR.)

15. Row-level and column-level security: how do you implement them? RLS: policy/filter bound to role or session attributes (Snowflake row access policies, BigQuery row access policies, Postgres RLS, Unity Catalog filters; check current docs). CLS: policy tags/masking policies so unauthorized roles see masked or null values. Manage via groups, not users; test policies; audit access. Follow-ups: Performance cost? (Predicate pushdown/caching differences.) Derived tables leaking? (Policies don't follow copies: enforce at the source, propagate tags via lineage.)

16. How do you test a data pipeline? Unit tests for transformations; integration tests on a small dataset in an ephemeral environment; contract tests at boundaries; idempotency and backfill-equivalence tests; data diff between old and new logic on a sample; production data-quality tests continue after deploy. Follow-ups: Test data with PII? (Synthetic or masked; never prod PII in dev.) Testing time logic? (Inject clock; boundary and timezone cases.)

17. How do you verify a backfill or migration? Shadow-write to a new table; compare counts, key sums, min/max dates, distinct keys; partition-level checksums to localize diffs; row-level diff on mismatches; define tolerances (late data, rounding); swap atomically; keep rollback. Follow-ups: Equal counts but different data? (Checksums catch it.) Reconciling against a moving source? (Snapshot/as-of both sides.)

18. How do you handle duplicates in a warehouse where PKs are not enforced? Detect with GROUP BY HAVING COUNT>1 on the business key per snapshot; prevent by idempotent loads (MERGE/upsert, dedupe with ROW_NUMBER over latest ingestion), natural/surrogate key strategy, unique tests in CI/prod; investigate source of duplicates (at-least-once delivery, retries). Follow-ups: Which row wins? (Latest by event time then ingestion time, documented rule.) Late duplicates? (Dedupe window or full-key MERGE.)

19. What goes in a data catalog and what makes it succeed? Technical metadata (schema, stats, lineage), business context (glossary, owner, description, certification), usage/popularity, quality status and tags (PII). Success needs automated ingestion from code/orchestrator, enforced ownership, and integration in workflows (access requests, alerts). Follow-ups: Build vs buy? (OpenMetadata/DataHub vs commercial; depends on team capacity.) How to keep docs fresh? (Docs-as-code, CI requires descriptions.)

20. Where does data cost go and how do you cut it? Storage, compute (scans, shuffles, always-on clusters), egress, streaming infra, and people-time on reprocessing. Cut with partition pruning, incremental processing, compaction, right-sizing/auto-suspend, lifecycle tiers, retention, eliminating unused assets, query guardrails, and showback. Challenge freshness SLAs that drive cost. Follow-ups: How to find waste? (Query/billing logs by table, cost per pipeline, access-recency report.) Tradeoff with reliability? (Retries/duplicated environments cost money: tier by criticality.)

21. What is MDM and how does it relate to quality? Managing shared master entities (customer/product) with matching, merge/survivorship rules, and cross-reference IDs to produce a golden record. It attacks duplication and inconsistency at the root, supported by stewardship and governance. Follow-ups: Deterministic vs probabilistic matching? (Exact keys vs scored fuzzy matches with review thresholds.) How to handle unmerge? (Keep source records and match history.)

22. Who owns data quality? Producers own correctness at the source and the contract; the data platform provides tooling and defaults; consumers define fitness-for-use and report issues; stewards arbitrate definitions. "Everyone" with no named owner means no one. Follow-ups: How to get producers to care? (Contracts in CI, quality metrics visible to their leadership, incident attribution.)
